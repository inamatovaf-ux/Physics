Differential Equations in Physical Processes

How a simple rule about rate of change predicts cooling coffee and salty water.

1. The big idea

Many physical laws do not tell us the quantity itself — they tell us how fast it changes. A rate of change is a derivative, so the law becomes a differential equation.

Step 1. Read the physics: what is the rate of change proportional to?
Step 2. Write it as an equation with a derivative.
Step 3. Separate the variables and integrate.
Step 4. Use the given data to find the constants.
dy/dt = k · y  ⟹  y = C · ekt

2. Example A — Newton's law of cooling

Problem 80. A body cooled in 10 minutes from 100° to 60°. The surrounding air is kept at 20°. When will the body cool to 25°? (Rate of cooling is proportional to the difference between body and air temperature.)

Model. Let T(t) be the body temperature, 20° the air temperature:

dT/dt = −k (T − 20)

Solution.

  1. Separate variables: dT/(T − 20) = −k dt
  2. Integrate: ln(T − 20) = −kt + ln C, so T − 20 = C·e−kt
  3. At t = 0, T = 100: C = 80. So T = 20 + 80·e−kt
  4. At t = 10, T = 60: 40 = 80·e−10k, so e−10k = 1/2. The temperature difference halves every 10 minutes.
  5. We need T = 25: 5 = 80·(1/2)t/10, so (1/2)t/10 = 1/16 = (1/2)4, hence t/10 = 4.
T(t) = 20 + 80 · (1/2)t/10
Answer: after 40 minutes

3. Example B — Mixing problem (salt in a tank)

Problem 78. A tank holds 100 L of solution containing 10 kg of salt. Water flows in continuously at 5 L/min and is stirred in; the mixture flows out at the same rate. How much salt remains after one hour?
General mixture formula: dx/dt = cin·rin − (x/V)·rout

x — amount of salt (kg); V — volume of liquid in the tank (L); cin — concentration of the incoming liquid (kg/L); rin, rout — flow rates in and out (L/min). Idea: rate of change of salt = salt coming in − salt going out.

Step 1 — put in the numbers. Inflow equals outflow (5 L/min), so the volume stays V = 100 L.

PartFormulaValue
Salt INcin·rin0 × 5 = 0 kg/min (pure water)
Salt OUT(x/V)·rout(x/100) × 5 = 0.05x kg/min

Step 2 — write the equation. Salt IN minus salt OUT:

dx/dt = 0 − 5 · (x/100) = −0.05 x

Solution.

  1. Separate variables (x on the left, t on the right): dx/x = −0.05 dt
  2. Integrate both sides: ln x = −0.05t + ln C. Remove the logarithm: x = C·e−0.05t
  3. Use the start: at t = 0 there are 10 kg, so 10 = C·e0 and C = 10.
  4. One hour = 60 min: x = 10·e−0.05·60 = 10·e−3 ≈ 10 × 0.0498 ≈ 0.50 kg.
x(t) = 10 · e−0.05 t
Answer: ≈ 0.5 kg of salt (about 5% of the original)

Meaning of k = 0.05 = r/V = 5/100: every minute 5% of the liquid is replaced. The amount of salt halves about every ln 2 / 0.05 ≈ 14 minutes.

4. What both problems share

Both obey the same pattern: the rate of change is proportional to the current "excess" (temperature difference or amount of salt). The solution is always an exponential, and the quantity approaches its limit (20° or 0 kg) but never overshoots it.

dy/dt = −k (y − y∞)  ⟹  y = y∞ + (y0 − y∞) e−kt

Same mathematics also describes radioactive decay, discharging capacitors and drug elimination from the body.
