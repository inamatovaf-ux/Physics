Exponential Growth Problem

Lesson 31–32 · Applied problems using integration and differentiation · Differential equations

Problem statement

A bacterial colony initially contains 100 bacteria and begins to grow at a rate proportional to its size. After 1 hour the number of bacteria reaches 420.

Find:
a) an expression for the number of bacteria after t hours;
b) the number of bacteria after 3 hours;
c) the growth rate after 3 hours;
d) the time when the number of bacteria reaches 10 000.

Illustration of the condition

Each dot is one bacterium (drawn to scale): in one hour the colony grows 4.2 times.

Step 5–7. Solution

1
Separate the variables (the equation is separable):
dN/dt = kN ⟹ dN/N = k dt ⟹ ∫dN/N = ∫k dt ⟹ ln N = kt + c
N(t) = C·ekt, where C = ec
2
Use the initial condition N(0) = 100:
C·e0 = 100 ⟹ C = 100
3
Use the additional condition N(1) = 420:
100·ek = 420 ⟹ ek = 4.2 ⟹ k = ln 4.2 ≈ 1.435
4
Particular solution
N(t) = 100·ekt = 100·(4.2)t

a) N(t) = 100·4.2t = 100·e1.435t
b) N(3) = 100·4.2³ = 100·74.088 ≈ 7409 bacteria
c) Growth rate v(t) = N′(t) = kN(t).
v(3) = 1.435·7408.8 ≈ 10 632 bacteria per hour
d) 100·4.2t = 10 000 ⟹ 4.2t = 100 ⟹ t = ln 100 / ln 4.2 = 4.605 / 1.435 ≈ 3.21 h (about 3 h 13 min)
